[1] SYNOPSIS / ABSTRACT
-----------------------------------------------------------------
CityShield / VisionScan is a single AI policing platform that answers five of
your Category-II problem statements at once, instead of five separate prototypes.
One codebase, one database, one security layer, one live deployment.

Its flagship module does GIS crime-hotspot mapping and predictive patrol routing:
a recency-weighted risk model (exponential decay + NCRB-derived priors + a live
anomaly boost) ranks city zones, and a nearest-neighbour + 2-opt optimiser turns
the top zones into an efficient patrol route. Around it sit four more modules on
the same data: always-on CCTV anomaly detection (fire/smoke/accident/weapon/
violence) via CLIP + YOLOv8, an AI legal-documentation assistant (CrimeGPT) that
suggests BNS/BNSS sections and drafts FIR-ready documents, a unified legal &
government-intelligence search, and a structured cyber-fraud intake with a
"golden-hour 1930" prompt.

The modules form one real closed loop: a live CCTV anomaly auto-creates a
geo-tagged case, raises that locality's predictive risk, and dispatches the
nearest patrol unit. It runs live today over HTTPS, CPU-only, offline-capable,
with no paid AI APIs — hosted at zero infrastructure cost on Microsoft Azure
for Students.

[2] LITERATURE REVIEW / EXISTING INNOVATION & TECHNOLOGY
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Predictive policing exists commercially — PredPol/Geolitica, HunchLab and
Palantir use historical-incident models to forecast risk; ShotSpotter does
acoustic gunshot detection. CCTV analytics (Briefcam, AnyVision) and commercial
face recognition (NEC, Clearview) handle video. Legal-AI tools (CaseMine,
SpotDraft) draft documents. These work, but they are proprietary, expensive,
cloud/GPU-dependent, and largely opaque — predictive systems in particular have
faced fairness and "black-box" criticism (e.g. COMPAS), and most are five
separate products that do not share data.

Our gaps-to-fill: (a) transparency — every hotspot has a "Why this hotspot?"
breakdown and the predictive model is backtested with rolling-origin cross-
validation rather than asserted; (b) cost & sovereignty — CPU-only, offline-
capable, no paid APIs, deployable on commodity hardware a department already
owns; (c) integration — one platform where detection, prediction, dispatch and
documentation share a single database, instead of five disconnected tools. We
build on open models (OpenAI CLIP, Ultralytics YOLOv8, ArcFace, MiniLM/ChromaDB
RAG) and open data standards (OpenStreetMap, NCRB statistics).

[3] YOUR APPROACH TO SOLVE THE PROBLEM
-----------------------------------------------------------------
One unified platform, modular by problem statement, on a shared data model.

Prediction: city zones are scored by a recency-weighted model — recent incidents
count more via exponential half-life decay — blended with NCRB-derived category
priors and a real-time boost when an anomaly is detected nearby. The ranked zones
feed a nearest-neighbour + 2-opt routing optimiser that outputs an efficient
patrol path. We do NOT just claim accuracy: a rolling-origin backtest reports
Hit-Rate@k, PAI (Predictive Accuracy Index), capture rate and bootstrap
confidence intervals, against frequency / prior / random baselines.

Detection: each CCTV/video frame is scored by CLIP zero-shot against
threat/normal prompts (with measured-margin calibration and debounce to cut false
alarms); YOLOv8 adds object labels and localized fire/smoke boxes. Optional
ArcFace face matching for watchlists.

Documentation & intelligence: CrimeGPT maps a case's facts to BNS/BNSS sections
and drafts documents; a ChromaDB + MiniLM RAG search covers legal/government
corpora; Gemini is optional and falls back to grounded offline templates so the
system never depends on a paid API. All wrapped in JWT/RBAC auth, OWASP-Top-10
hardening and audit logging, with a 77-test regression suite. Stack: FastAPI +
SQLite backend, React/Vite frontend, Docker Compose, deployed on Azure behind
Caddy auto-HTTPS.

[6] CHALLENGES / RISKS IN IMPLEMENTING THE FINAL PROTOTYPE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
- Data access: the demo runs on SYNTHETIC data (no real people/cases). Real
  deployment needs an MoU / data-sharing agreement for actual incident records;
  we built the model to ingest standard incident schemas to ease that.
- Fairness & accountability: predictive policing can entrench bias. We mitigate
  with full transparency ("Why this hotspot?" score breakdown), human-in-the-loop
  dispatch (the system suggests, officers decide), and published backtest metrics.
- CPU latency: to stay cheap and offline, inference is CPU-only — a CCTV clip
  takes ~30-90 s to process. A production deployment can add a GPU for real-time.
- Privacy & law: face matching and CCTV analytics must comply with the DPDP Act
  and due process; these are opt-in modules with access controls and audit logs.
- Licensing: YOLOv8 is AGPL-3.0 — documented in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES; production
  can swap in a permissive detector. The rest of our stack is permissive/MIT.
- Scale & accuracy drift: models need periodic retraining on fresh local data;
  rolling-origin backtesting is built in to monitor drift.
